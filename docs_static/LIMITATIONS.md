# Limitations

This file lists what the archive cannot certify. Every item below is a recorded limitation, not a number to be inferred.

## Vendor internals that are unobservable
- **cuSOLVER IRS (`cusolver_irs_*`)**: the requested main/lowest precisions (FP64/FP16, FP64/FP32, FP32/FP16) are documented inputs, not a complete precision tuple. The internal factorization panel arithmetic, factor storage, triangular-solve precision, residual precision and the vendor stopping rule are not observable. Fallback to a main-precision factorization is visible only through the sign of `niters` (negative = did not converge; with fallback enabled the returned X comes from the main-precision solve). Stage costs of a fallback are not exposed; such records are policy successes at best, never intrinsic low-precision successes. BF16 and TF32 lowest precisions are rejected on sm_70 (status 4) and were not used.
- **MAGMA 2.9.0 (`magma_dx_*`, `magma_ht_*`, `magma_lu*`)**: the driver structure (`src/dxgesv_gmres_gpu.cpp`) was read: FP64 residuals, FP32 triangular solves (IRSTRS/GMSTRS), FP32-stored factors, `magma_xshgetrf_gpu` with FP16-input/FP32-output tensor-core trailing updates and CPU (OpenBLAS, 8 threads) panels. Kernel-level arithmetic is not audited. `magma_dhgesv_iteref_gpu`/`magma_dsgesv_iteref_gpu` are GMRES-based (they call the GMSTRS mode), not classical IR. The GMRES modes returned an all-zero X for nrhs>1 while reporting success; these records are recorded as unsupported. One SHT+IRSTRS run returned NaN after 208 iterations while reporting `info=0, iter=208`; it is recorded as a nonfinite numerical failure. For n not larger than MAGMA's half-precision block size (128 at n<=6000 per `magma_get_hgetrf_nb`), `magma_htgetrf_gpu` performs an FP32 factorization (no FP16 update), so MAGMA "half" results at n<=128 are FP32 results.
- **cuSOLVER Xgetrf**: internal blocking and panel arithmetic unknown (FP32/FP64 operands documented).
- Host-panel work: all MAGMA hybrid routines and `magma_htgetrf_gpu` use CPU panels through OpenBLAS limited to 8 threads on cores 0-7 (deliberate, recorded); their timings depend on that CPU configuration.

## Custom implementation limits
- The custom heterogeneous LU (`hetero16_*`) uses cuSOLVER `Xgetrf` on tall n×512 panels. Its FP32 same-code-path control (`custom32_*`) was **1.55× slower than cuSOLVER `Xgetrf` FP32 at n=8192** on development data (0.098 s vs 0.0635 s) and 1.44× at n=4096: it narrowly fails the project's 1.5× promotion gate at 8192. The overhead is the panel path (tall-skinny panel calls plus row-swap and conversion kernels), not the trailing GEMM; a MAGMA native recursive panel was tried on development data (8% gain, different `info` semantics) and not adopted. Interpretation of `hetero16_*` timings is therefore restricted to "custom heterogeneous implementation with a known panel overhead"; the MAGMA and cuSOLVER mixed drivers are the mature compiled endpoints.
- GMRES uses one-pass modified Gram-Schmidt on the device; `||I - V^T V||_F` was not measured. Happy breakdown is verified with the true residual. The left-preconditioned "paper-aligned" variants run a single cycle (no restart) with m=40 and are controls at moderate n only; the basis is FP64 (not the paper's working precision FP32), so they are not an exact reproduction of Carson–Higham Algorithm 8.1 / Higham 2019 Algorithm 3.1.
- The cost-aware recovery policy is an experimental heuristic calibrated with in-run stage timings and a fixed ratio (3×) for the next factorization; it is not Oktay–Carson multistage refinement.
- Stopping: the production rule is backward-error based (η and ω on the original system, FP64 evaluation) and stops FP32-working methods at the initial solve on well-conditioned FP32 inputs (η already below 100 u32). Forward accuracy under that rule is κ·u32-level, not u32-level. The attainable-accuracy floors are in suite E2a (unattainable tolerance, stagnation/max_outer rule), which is a diagnostic track, not a time-to-target policy.

## Residual-floor ambiguity and references
- Original-system η and ω are evaluated in FP64 with FMA accumulation from the stored inputs; a double-double recomputation of every saved solution is in `tests/audit/audit_results.json` (`eta_dd`). Classification uses the FP64 evaluator; the audit shows the FP64/DD agreement per solution. No `indeterminate` backward classification was issued automatically; disputed cases can be re-classified from the stored numerators.
- Forward errors for general families use `numerical_reference` solutions (cuSOLVER FP64 LU, or FP32 LU when the FP64 factor buffer did not fit, plus double-double residual refinement). Their uncertainty proxy is `reference_resid_dd_rel × kappa2_estimate`; forward qualification is marked `indeterminate` whenever that proxy exceeds 10% of the threshold. The dyadic family (`exact_sign`, `unit_column` RHS) has `exact_stored` references; `unit_column` references contain zero components (count recorded). E1 cases use 100-digit mpmath references rechecked at 120 digits.
- `kappa2_estimate` is a 12-step power/inverse-iteration estimate with the reference factors; it is not a certified condition number. For FP32 stored problems at nominal κ≥1e8 the stored spectrum need not match the nominal one.
- Growth proxy is max|LU buffer| / max|scaled A|, not the maximal intermediate element growth; vendor factor internals report no growth.

## Timing limits
- GPU clocks were not locked (no privileges); boost behaviour varies. Timings at n≤2048 (E2/E3/E6 small cases) ran while a light audit process intermittently used the GPU for regeneration hashing; E4 timings did not.
- Repetitions were reduced for the 4-hour limit (7 at n≤8192, 5 at 16384, 3 at ≥24576; host-mode 2/1) and seeds were reduced to 2 (n≤8192) or 1 (n≥12288) in E4. Repeated timings of one matrix are not independent matrix samples; speedup statistics over one seed are descriptive only.
- E4 at n≥32768 ran only the core method group (plus IRS/MAGMA groups at 32768 FP64) and only the geometric κ=1e2 family (24576 also clustered κ=1e4); nrhs=8 only at n≤8192 and n=16384 geometric. 12288 ran with one seed. These are explicit skips in coverage.csv, not failures.
- Host-to-host times include the H2D copy of A from pinned host memory and the D2H copy of X; device-ready times include restoring A into the factor buffer. Common workspace/handle setup is excluded uniformly.
- No kernel-level profiler traces (nsys not installed). Profiles are instrumented stage timers and solver counters (profiles/).

## Omitted or reduced suites
- E1 n=512 was not attempted (conditional). E2 extra FP64 κ∈{1e10,1e12} only for the geometric spectrum. No KKT family; RBF is the application-like family. Wilkinson cases at n≤512 (FP64) and n≤128 (FP32) only.
- E3 initial/correction RHS normalization ablation is not applicable to the GPU track (FP32 triangular solves); it remains in the E1 scalar track only.
- E5 reuse records exist for the custom-engine methods only; cuSOLVER IRS and MAGMA drivers refactor on every call and are recorded as fresh solves.
- 32768 was run (conditional size) for FP64 and FP32; 36864 (10.1 GiB FP64) and 49152 (9.06 GiB FP32) ran for the core group with one seed.
- The number of independent seeds per cell is small everywhere (≤3); no confidence intervals are claimed.
- The frozen configuration file was amended twice after the first evaluation records (plan trimming and the E2a track); see configs/FROZEN_CONFIG_HISTORY.md. No solver parameter of a reported method changed after evaluation started.
- Development-phase trials were produced by an earlier binary differing only by the absence of the unused `--panel magma` code path (build/BUILD_HISTORY.md).

## Process notes affecting record provenance
- The budget-policy ladder (`policy_*_budget*`) had a bug in the first E6 pass (escalated stages returned immediately after Krylov budget exhaustion). The driver was fixed and rebuilt before E4 started; the affected E6 run directory was deleted and E6 was re-run in full with the fixed binary. No other suite used budget-policy methods. The dev/E2 binaries differ from the final one only by that fix and by the unused `--panel magma` path (see build/BUILD_HISTORY.md).
- Two orchestrator restarts occurred (queue interruption while fixing the above); completed jobs are skipped via done-markers, so no job ran twice; per-job stderr tails and return codes are in logs/jobs/.
- Light GPU use by the regeneration audit overlapped the E2 and the first part of E6 (n≤16384) timings; E4 timings were taken with the GPU otherwise idle.
- Declared time-budget skips (markers with rc=-2, listed in coverage.csv as `skipped_time_budget`, written at 22:27 before those jobs started): E4 FP32 n=32768 (all method groups) and FP32 n=16384 nrhs=8; E5 n=16384; E3 n=8192 selected pairs. The FP32 ladder therefore has 8192, 16384 (nrhs=1) and 49152 only.
- The driver's memory-admission check was corrected at 22:59 (it had ignored already-allocated reusable buffers and skipped `native_lu64`/`native_ir64_w64_r64` at n=36864 in the first pass); those two methods were re-run at 36864 in a separate job with the corrected binary. Jobs after 22:59 (FP32 ladder, E2a, E3, E5, E8, the 24576 vendor groups) used the corrected binary; the change does not touch numerical code (build/BUILD_HISTORY.md).
- The refinement engine returns the LAST iterate, never a best-residual or reference-selected iterate. In the attainable-accuracy track (E2a) and in production runs that end by stagnation after divergence (FP16-factor stationary IR at κ≥1e4), the returned X can be far worse than the initial solve; those records are failures by design and are kept as such (e.g. E2a FP32 κ=1e4 `hetero16_ir_*` forward errors ~1e9).
- Range/scaling (E3) at n=2048, κ=1e4: the FP16-factor stationary methods fail for every scaling variant (global, none, two-sided), so E3 separates range effects only for the FP32-factor and vendor methods, which passed all global-exponent and row/column-span cases.
